# Second life: assessment, matchmaking and re-pricing

## What it does

A returned item is assessed: every way to relist it (as returned, or refurbished to each higher condition) with its refurbishment plan, market value, second-life price (D86: the market value, nothing added), whether the refurbishment pays for itself, and net rent per term, then the waiting list is ranked by D13 and the item is relisted in the winner's condition at its second-life price. The admin's "Assess and relist" on `/[locale]/assets/[id]/second-life` writes it; the page is back office (D85): only the admin sees it or any link to it, because it shows the whole waiting list; the quote, the price page and the signing then price the item on that amount, and the next contract names the one before it (D16), so the passport shows the chain of lives.

## The rule

1. **Options.** For each target condition from the returned one up to like new: `refurbishmentPlan` (the data's fixed steps whose `when` holds, then the condition upgrades), the market value after it (`marketValueCents`: net list price at the first lease × curve at the age × condition multiplier after × usage adjustment on the usage since the last battery replacement after the plan (D83, the organisers’ answer of 6 Nov, 10:35: a new battery resets it to 0; the total usage never resets and stays on the asset for the passport and circular impact)), and **D86: second-life price = the market value after the plan**, nothing added, then the normal band and factor through `priceQuote` for every allowed term (D43), no down payment. The refurbishment is the lessor's cost: the value after the plan already counts the improvement, so adding the cost on top (D12) charged the customer twice.
2. **Refurbish or not (D86, the organisers' comparison).** For each option above the as-returned one: the value gain over the as-returned option against the extra cost over its plan, "worth it" when gain > cost (`compareToAsReturned`); a tie is not worth the work, as the decision keeps the lower condition on a tie. The mandatory steps are in every plan, so they cancel.
3. **Verdicts (D62).** Each entry of the waiting list, the first failing step named as a code: `other_category`, `already_matched` (an earlier assessment won it), `expired` (`needed_by` before the assessment day), `term_not_offered`, `condition_unreachable`, `rent_not_priced`, `over_budget { shortCents }`, else `candidate`. An entry is offered the lowest condition that satisfies it: max(as returned, its minimum, fair).
4. **Ranking (D13).** No refurbishment beyond the mandatory steps first, then the earliest `needed_by`, then the highest total rent over the term (rent × term), then the lower id. Each row names the step that put it below the row above.
5. **Decision (D61, D86).** Relist in the winner's condition. With no winner (D86), the option with the largest net gain (gain − extra cost) among those the catalogue may lease: at or above max(as returned, good), the cost table's note, and priced on at least one term; the lower condition on a tie, so as returned when nothing is positive. The chosen option can still lose money when a cheaper one cannot be leased: laptop-006 is relisted like new at −1 756 because good (27 577) is under band A's minimum. With no winner and no such option at all, the decision is `relist_unpriced`: the assessment is refused (422, the code written from the message files) and the item stays returned, never relisted at a condition nothing can price. The mandatory steps are always done.

**After pulling D86**, run `npm run db:reset` or re-assess the returned items: `second-life-price.ts` quotes from the latest stored assessment, so a database seeded or assessed before D86 keeps quoting the D12 amount (market value + refurbishment cost) until then.

### Card 9: `ebike-007`, fair, 18 400 km, assessed on 2026-11-06

- **Value inputs (D60).** One past life, no contract in our books: the earliest list price, 549 000 (v1), aged by D11 from January 2023: 46 months. The e-bike curve between 36 (40 %) and 48 (31 %) gives 40 − 9 × 10/12 = **32.5 %**.
- **The plan, as returned:** battery 35 000 (18 400 ≥ 15 000 km since the last battery; the pack records none, so that is the total) + safety inspection 4 500 = **39 500**. To good: + fair→good 12 000 = **51 500**. To like new: + good→like new 8 000 = 59 500.
- **Two usage counters (D83).** The total, 18 400 km, never resets: the relisted bike keeps it, the passport shows it and the next exit must report at least it. The usage since the last battery is 0 after the plan; it is what the value's usage adjustment counts and what the next battery waits for (15 000 km more, so at 33 400 km on the odometer).
- **Market value (D83, the organisers' worked example):** before the battery 549 000 × 32.5 % × 0.65 × 0.80 (−20 % cap) = 92 781; with the new battery fair × 1.00 = **115 976** (1 159,76 €); good × 0.85 × 1.00 = **151 661**; like new × 0.95 = 169 504.
- **D86 price (the market value):** fair **115 976**; good **151 661**; like new **169 504**; all band A (30 000 to 199 999). Before D86 (D12, value + work): 155 476 (A), 203 161 (B), 229 004 (B).
- **Rents:** fair **6 497** for 12 months (115 976 × 0.056022 = 6 497.20; 24: 4 249, 36: 3 320, 48: 2 778); good **5 556** for 24 months (151 661 × 0.036637 = 5 556.40; 12: 8 496); like new **4 852** for 36 months (169 504 × 0.028625 = 4 852.05).
- **Refurbish or not, the organisers' comparison (D86).** Against the as-returned option (value 115 976 after the mandatory 39 500): to good the value gains 151 661 − 115 976 = **35 685** for 51 500 − 39 500 = **12 000** more work, **worth it** (net +23 685); to like new it gains **53 528** for **20 000** more, **worth it** (net +33 528). Their own framing from the bike before the battery: 151 661 − 92 781 = 58 880 against the 51 500 of the whole plan (35 000 + 12 000 + 4 500).
- **The waiting list (e-bikes):**

| wait-02 | fair, 12 m, ≤ 9 000     | fair       | 6 497 | candidate            | 1, winner                                  |

- **The answer.** Refurbish? **No**, only the mandatory battery and inspection (39 500): going to good would be worth it on its own numbers, but the winner takes it fair, and D61 relists in the winner's condition. Who? **`wait-02`**: both twins fit the budget and need it by 2026-11-10, but `wait-02` takes it as returned and `wait-13` needs 12 000 more of work, so D13's first step decides. D86 moved the numbers, not the winner: the rents fell (8 710 → 6 497, 7 219 → 5 556); the one verdict that changed is `wait-03`, now in budget (4 852 ≤ 6 000, was 317 over) and third. - **With nobody waiting** (D86), the bike would be relisted like new: the largest net gain, +33 528 against good's +23 685 (D61 relisted it good).

### Card 10: `ebike-008`, two past lives, good, 9 800 km

- 179 900 (v1) × 26 % (58 months, between 48 = 31 % and 60 = 25 %) × 0.85 × 82 % (9 steps of 1 000 km, −18 %) = 32 601.48 → **32 601**; the plan is the inspection only (9 800 < 15 000 km): **4 500**, our cost; D86 price **32 601** (band A; 37 101 under D12). ## Decisions

D86 (the price is the market value; refurbish or not by value gain against extra cost; the no-winner relist), D12 (re-pricing, its price replaced by D86), D77 (the inspection wins over the report), D13 (matchmaking), D15 (CO2), D16 and D66 (the contract chain), D22 and D23 (`returned` → `available`, statuses parsed before the write), D31 and D60 (the price and age the value starts from), D61 (refurbish or not, the floor), D62 (the budget test), D63 (where the price lives), D64 (the admin button, not an event handler), D65 (an assessment without a contract), D67 (a relisted fair item is open to anyone), D83 (two usage counters: the total never resets, the usage since the last battery takes the value's usage adjustment and the next battery; replaces D79, which had replaced D68), D85 (the page is back office: admin only).

## Pure functions

- `assessReturn(input, { curves, costs, pricing })` in `src/domain/second-life/assess.ts`. In: asset id, category, returned condition and total usage, the total at the last battery replacement (`usageAtLastReset`, D83; absent: none), the curve-age input (D11/D31), the new price (D60), the ISO day. Out: `{ assetId, category, isoDate, ageMonths, returnedCondition, options[] }`, each option `{ condition, upgrade, plan, marketValue, secondLifeFinancedCents, comparison, rents[{ termMonths, monthlyNetCents | null, band, refusal }] }`; `comparison` is null on the as-returned option.
- `compareToAsReturned({ valueCents, costCents }, { valueCents, costCents }) → { valueGainCents, extraCostCents, netGainCents, worthIt }` in `assess.ts` (D86).
- `matchDemand(assessment, demand)` in `src/domain/second-life/match.ts`. Out: `{ verdicts[{ demandId, neededBy, outcome: { code, params } }], ranking[{ …, rank, decidedBy }], winner, decision: { code, params }, relist }`.
- `firstLeaseCurve({ assetId, modelYear, previousLives, contracts, priceLists, today }) → { listPriceCents, age } | null` and `livesSoFar(packPreviousLives, contracts) → { previousLives, currentLife }` in `src/domain/second-life/first-lease.ts`: D60 in one place, read by the assessment and the exit (review findings 1 and 3); `firstLeaseListPriceCents` lives there too (re-exported from `assess.ts`). `withSecondLifePrice(pricing, assetId, cents)` in `assess.ts`.
- **The inspection (D77).** `POST /api/assets/[id]/assessments` takes an optional body `{ condition?, usageValue? }`: the admin's inspection, assessed and relisted on instead of the reported values the exit stored; no body assesses the stored values, as the assess button does. A per-block service (the farm machine's) counts from the usage of the newest assessment that did it.
- **The usage since the last battery (D83)** in `src/domain/refurbishment/plan.ts`: `usageSinceReset(total, atLastReset) → number` (none on record is the total; a total below the reading counts from 0) and `usageAtLastReset(assessments, category, costs) → number` (the usage of the newest assessment whose plan did a `resets_usage` step, else 0). `refurbishmentPlan` takes `usageAtLastReset` and returns `usageAfter` (the total, unchanged) and `usageSinceResetAfter` (0 when a step resets it). Stored as no column: the assessment rows already record each replacement.

## The bug report of 2026-11-06 (not reproduced)

A tester reported the second-life page broken and "Assess and relist" failing for an item returned through the app's own exit (leased, then "Give it back"). On a migrated database neither reproduces: every e-bike and laptop in the pack, leased and returned on the day it was signed, seven months in and at the end of term, as reported or worn, previews and assesses through the route as the admin (201); the regression tests above pin that path. The tester's dev server logged `The column main.AuditLog.prev_hash does not exist in the current database` (P2022): the audit signing (D81, #69) added columns, and a database not migrated after the pull fails every audited write, the assessment included. **If a page errors on a missing column after pulling, run `npx prisma migrate deploy` and restart the dev server.**

## Open questions

- D60 for seeded items assumes v1 is the price as new; the pack has no first-lease date or price. Asked as question 3 in [mentor-questions.md](../mentor-questions.md).
- The assessment is made on the button; a `UNIT_RETURNED` handler could make it automatic (D64).
- `wait-06` and `wait-14` (the laptop twins) differ only in their sign-up day, which the data does not hold; D13 then falls back to the lower id, `wait-06`, which matches the brief's answer.
- D86 makes a cheap item's price its value alone, so some fall under band A's 30 000 minimum and cannot be leased as returned (`laptop-006` good, 27 577); the no-winner relist then takes the cheapest condition that can be priced. Whether such an item should go to a buyer instead is open.
- A later life gets the same category cap as a first lease (D43: `allowedTerms` in `assess.ts`, `matchDemand`); whether it should get a shorter one (an old item on a 48-month lease outlives its design life) is still open.
